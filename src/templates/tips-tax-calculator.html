What if I'm married but we file separately?

Married couples who send in separate returns cannot claim this deduction at all. Tax forms call this your filing status. It does not change the $25,000 limit, which is one limit per return either way, never doubled, it only moves the income at which the break starts shrinking.

How the "no tax on tips" deduction works

If you work for tips, a 2025 law lets you take some of them off the income the federal government taxes you on. You can claim it whether or not you itemize, on top of your standard deduction — so for most tipped workers it is money off the top, not a trade against something else you were already claiming.

It comes off after your adjusted gross income is worked out, so it does not lower that figure itself. That matters for one reason only: the income phase-out further down is measured on it.

The law is the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 2025, which added Internal Revenue Code §224 for qualified tips; the deduction is claimed on Schedule 1-A.

Annual cap. Up to $25,000 of qualified tips per year, for tax years 2025 through 2028. This is a single per-return limit — it is not doubled for married couples filing jointly.

Which tips qualify. Cash and charged tips (cash, check, card, or tokens exchangeable for cash) paid voluntarily by the customer, in an occupation that "customarily and regularly" receives tips — the Treasury has published the list of qualifying occupations, and you can check whether your job is on it. Mandatory service charges (an automatic 18% on a large party, for example) are not tips and don't count.

Income phase-out. The deduction shrinks by $100 for every $1,000 your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) is over $150,000 (single) or $300,000 (joint), reaching zero at $400,000 / $550,000.

It's a deduction, not a refund. It lowers your taxable income, so the dollar you save is roughly the deduction times your marginal tax rate. And it's income-tax only: Social Security and Medicare still apply to tips, and you still report all your tips as normal — you claim the deduction when you file.

A worked example: Renata, a restaurant server

Renata is single. Last year she earned about $52,000 in all — $34,000 in wages plus $18,000 in tips. Here's how the deduction plays out:

  • Qualified tips: her $18,000 of cash and card tips is below the $25,000 cap, and her $52,000 income is far under the $150,000 phase-out line, so all $18,000 is deductible.
  • Federal tax saved: that $18,000 comes off income taxed in her 12% bracket, cutting her federal income tax by about $2,160 ($18,000 × 12%).
  • What doesn't change: Social Security and Medicare were still owed on all $18,000 of tips, and she still reports every tip as usual — the $2,160 shows up as a bigger refund when she files.

Remember the cap is a flat $25,000 per return, not per person — a married couple filing jointly still shares one $25,000 limit, it isn't doubled.

Who can claim it

You need a Social Security number valid for employment, and if you're married you must file jointly (married filing separately is not eligible). Both employees (tips on your W-2) and self-employed workers can claim it — the self-employed are limited to their net business income, and specified service businesses are excluded. You must work in one of the occupations the Treasury lists as customarily tipped — look up your occupation and its Box 14b code here.

Common questions

Are all my tips tax-free now? No. Only up to $25,000 is deductible, only for federal income tax, and only for 2025–2028. Social Security and Medicare still apply, and your state may still tax tips.

Do mandatory service charges count? No. An automatic gratuity or service charge added by the business is treated as wages, not a voluntary tip, so it doesn't qualify.

What is MAGI? Modified adjusted gross income is your adjusted gross income plus a few add-backs, mainly foreign-income exclusions — for almost everyone it's the same as your AGI. It's the income figure the phase-out above uses.

What is FICA? FICA is the Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) payroll tax withheld from your pay. It's separate from federal income tax, and this deduction doesn't reduce it — FICA is still owed on your tips.

What is filing status? It's the category you file under — single, married filing jointly, or head of household here. It sets your phase-out thresholds. Married workers must file jointly to claim this deduction; married filing separately can't.
