{{STATE_NAME}} take-home pay calculator

This is the figure before tax, not the amount that lands in your account

Your pay stub calls it gross pay. It is what you are paid before income tax, Social Security, Medicare, health cover or a retirement plan come out of it. A yearly salary or an hourly rate both work, the next question asks which one you typed.

Is that a yearly salary or an hourly rate?

Switching between the two converts the number you already typed

Pick the other one and the figure above is converted for you, using the hours a week on the next card. Nothing is lost, so it is safe to change your mind.

Your usual week, not a busy one

This turns your hourly rate into a year of pay, so use the hours you work most weeks. Overtime is not counted here: it is paid at a higher rate and it changes from week to week, so it belongs in the overtime calculator instead.

How often are you paid?

It changes the size of each check, not the tax you owe for the year

The yearly totals are the same either way. This only decides how the year is cut up, so the figure you see is the one you would actually receive.

How do you do your taxes?

It moves the income bands, so it changes the tax on the same pay

Tax forms call this your filing status. Filing together usually means a wider band of income is taxed at each rate, so the same salary keeps more of itself. This estimate assumes one income; if your husband or wife also earns, the real figure is higher than this.

Do you, or your husband or wife, turn 65 at any point this year?

Do you put part of your pay into a retirement plan at work?

Look on your pay stub for a line like 401(k), 403(b), 457, TSP, retirement or pension.

Does money come out of your pay for health insurance or a health account?

Look on your pay stub for lines like medical, dental, vision, HSA or FSA.

Do you claim any children or other dependents?

That is $0 of dependent credit for the year. A W-4 counts $2,000 for each child under 17 and $500 for each other dependent.

Did you ask your employer to take extra tax out of each paycheck?

Look on your pay stub for a line like additional or extra withholding. On the W-4 you handed in, it is the box on line 4(c).

If your W-4 shows a per-paycheck amount, multiply it by the number of paychecks you get in a year.

Is anything else taken out of your pay after tax?

Look near the bottom of your pay stub for lines like union dues, life insurance, a repayment or a garnishment.
