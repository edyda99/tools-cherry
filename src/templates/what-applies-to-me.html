Your income, roughly

Roughly what will be on line 11 of your federal return, your adjusted gross income?

That is your pay after pre-tax items like a 401(k) or health premiums, not your gross salary. If you are married and file together, use the figure for both of you. This only decides which limits we point out, and you can skip it.

These limits use modified adjusted gross income, which is not the same as salary, household pay, or the figure on your pay stub.

A deduction lowers the amount of your income that gets taxed. It is not money handed back to you, and it is not the same as a credit. Roughly, what a deduction is worth to you is the deduction amount multiplied by the rate your top dollars are taxed at.

Retirement income

Many states leave pension and retirement income out of state tax. We do not include any of that.

What this does not cover

  • We do not include state credits or personal exemptions.
  • We do not include how a state treats retirement, pension or Social Security income.
  • Our state data covers tips and overtime only. Do not assume a state treats the senior, SALT, car loan, charity or mortgage insurance rules the same way. We have no data on that.
  • If you lived in the state for only part of the year, or you pay tax there without living there, our numbers do not fit your situation.
  • Our state data stops at {{TAX_YEAR}}, so 2027 and 2028 are not covered.
  • Figures are as of the date each file was checked, not as of today. Our payroll and local tax figures were checked on {{PAY_DATE}}, and our state tips and overtime figures on {{OB_DATE}}. Some states change rates part way through a year.
  • This is general information, not tax advice. If you are unsure, check with a tax preparer or your state revenue department.
