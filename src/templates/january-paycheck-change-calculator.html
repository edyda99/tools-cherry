Why is my January paycheck different?

A first paycheck of the year is often smaller even with no change in pay: Social Security tax starts again, your 401(k) or health insurance takes more, or the tax tables change. Put in your pay to see which hit you, biggest first. Nothing is uploaded.

The five reasons a January paycheck changes

  1. Social Security tax starts again. Social Security tax is 6.2% of your pay, but only up to a yearly limit. In 2026 the limit is $184,500. If you earned more than that at one job, the tax stopped partway through the year, and your last paychecks were bigger. On January 1 the count starts from $0, so the 6.2% comes back on your first 2027 paycheck. The 2027 limit is {{WAGE_BASE_2027}}. The extra 0.9% Medicare tax on pay over $200,000 restarts its count on January 1 too, so it drops off your January paycheck. State disability, paid-leave and unemployment deductions with a yearly limit (in New Jersey and New York, for example) restart the same way and come back.
  2. You save more in your 401(k). Many people raise their savings rate for the new year. More saving means less take-home pay, but less of it than you might think, because the 401(k) money is not taxed now.
  3. Your health insurance costs more. Most employers set new prices on January 1. A higher premium takes more out of each paycheck, though it also lowers your taxes a little.
  4. The federal tax tables change. Each year the IRS raises the tax brackets and the standard deduction (the part of your income that is not taxed) for inflation. That usually means a little less federal tax in January.
  5. Your state's tax tables change. Some states change rates or deductions on January 1.

Your gross pay itself does not change in January unless you got a raise. If it did, that is a sixth reason, and you can see it by putting your new salary in the calculator.
