Which one should you actually use?

If you convert occasionally and want zero cost and zero signup: Tools Berry or PDF Edit. Both are genuinely free with no paid tier hiding behind the free one. Tools Berry converts on its server by default so it can read scanned pages, and one link under the button switches it to a browser mode that uploads nothing, while PDF Edit is browser-only, with no way to read a scan.

If your file is bigger than the free-tier caps allow, or you don't mind uploading it: Sejda and iLovePDF both have real free tiers, but with page/size caps that a $4-7.50/month plan removes. Read their free-tier limits above before assuming "free" covers your file.

If you're already paying for Adobe Acrobat: its online converter is a reasonable option, but the free tier requires a sign-in to download your file and gates unlimited use behind a subscription — worth comparing against a genuinely free alternative first if cost matters.

If privacy is the deciding factor: PDF Edit converts entirely in your browser, and Tools Berry does the same in one click, from a link under its Convert button. Its default converts on a server and deletes the file straight away. ExactPDF's marketing leads with "100% local," but its own disclosure shows Word↔PDF conversions specifically are the one exception that uses a server, and it never offers you the local path for that direction.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best alternatives to PDF to Word conversion apps and how do their pricing compare?

The main alternatives are PDF Edit, ExactPDF, Sejda, iLovePDF, and Adobe Acrobat. Tools Berry, PDF Edit, and ExactPDF's core tools are free with no paid tier. Sejda's free tier caps at 20 pages per Word conversion and 3 tasks per hour, with paid plans from $5 for a 7-day pass up to $7.50/month. iLovePDF's free tier caps PDF-to-Word and Word-to-PDF at 15 MB per task with 1 conversion per tool, with Premium at $4-7/month removing those caps. Adobe Acrobat's online tool is free for limited daily use but requires a free Adobe account to download the result, with unlimited use requiring a paid Acrobat plan.

What are the best alternatives to Word to PDF tools with pricing information available on the web?

For Word-to-PDF specifically: Tools Berry converts entirely in your browser for free, with no size-based paid tier. ExactPDF's Word-to-PDF runs through a temporary server (not local, despite marketing itself as privacy-first elsewhere) and is free with no published limit. Sejda and iLovePDF both upload your file to convert it, with the same free-tier caps and paid pricing listed above. Adobe Acrobat also uploads your file and gates unlimited use behind a paid subscription.

Is there a truly free PDF to Word or Word to PDF converter with no catch?

Tools Berry and PDF Edit are both free with no paid tier and no signup. The real difference between them: Tools Berry converts on a 25 MB server by default, which is what lets it read scanned pages, and still gives you a browser mode in one click. PDF Edit is browser-only, so a scan comes out empty.

Which of these run in your browser instead of uploading your file?

PDF Edit converts entirely in your browser. Tools Berry offers the same as a one-click option, though its default PDF-to-Word conversion runs on a server so it can read scans, and deletes your file straight away. ExactPDF markets itself as local-first but its own disclaimer states Office-format conversions (the Word-to-PDF and PDF-to-Word directions) run through a temporary server. Sejda, iLovePDF, and Adobe Acrobat all upload your file to a server for every conversion.
