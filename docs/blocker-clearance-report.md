# Blocker clearance report, branch feat/authority-moves (HEAD 71f9523)

## 1. Decision

DO NOT SHIP. Four remaining defects, all confirmed in built `dist` at HEAD: (a) Alaska and Washington paycheck pages still tell readers a bonus meets federal withholding and nothing else, contradicting those states' own bonus pages on this same branch; (b) the same two pages still carry the "No state income tax does not mean no state payroll deductions, check your stub" disclaimer directly above the new "Included in your take-home" line, so the page tells the reader the deduction is both excluded and included; (c) `/what-applies-to-me/` renders the garbled string "are exempt., $184,500 (2026 Social Security wage cap)" twice, caused by an unauthorized rewrite of Delaware payroll data; (d) all 51 state pages assert the study runs "the 2026 rules of every state and DC", a claim newly added on this branch and false for California and Oklahoma, which is the same false-sourcing class the byline fix was supposed to remove.

## 4. Remaining failures, by severity

### Blocker

1. **Washington "what applies to me" says a bonus is hit by federal withholding only.** `dist/washington-paycheck-calculator/index.html` renders "Bonuses: there is no Washington supplemental rate to apply, so only federal withholding touches the extra pay." WA PFML 0.807% and WA Cares 0.58% are both withheld from bonus wages. The same branch's `dist/washington-bonus-tax-calculator/index.html` says the opposite twice: "Washington also takes its paid family leave and long-term care contributions out of a bonus". Source `src/content/state-applies.js:67`, inside `bonusLine()`, which branches on `supp.method === 'none'` and never consults `state.employeePrograms`. That file does not appear in `git diff main...HEAD` at all.
2. **Alaska carries the same claim in different wording.** `dist/alaska-paycheck-calculator/index.html`: "Bonuses: a bonus meets federal withholding and nothing else, because Alaska has no supplemental rate." AK SUI employee share 0.50% up to the $54,200 base is withheld from bonus wages. Contradicted by `dist/alaska-bonus-tax-calculator/index.html` on the same branch. Source `src/content/state-applies.js:69`, third frame of the same `pickFrame` array. Blast radius confirmed as these two states only.
3. **AK and WA paycheck pages contradict themselves on whether the deduction is inside the take-home figure.** The unchanged `NOTAX_DISCLAIMER` at `build.js:1885` still renders "No state income tax does not mean no state payroll deductions. Check your stub for state-run leave or disability contributions." in the "What this estimate doesn't include" block, while the section below it says "Included in your take-home: the AK SUI (employee) contribution is subtracted from the take-home estimate at the top of this page." On main the disclaimer was true, because main's Alaska lead was $61,593 and the $271 was not subtracted. The branch changed the lead to $61,322 and did not update the disclaimer. Same on Washington. Verified by grep of built `dist`.

### Major

4. **Unauthorized rewrite of Delaware tax content.** `src/data/state-payroll-2026.json` has Delaware's `employeeRate2026` replaced with a prose paragraph on employer-size tiering and exemptions, `wageBaseOrCap` rewritten to add a new "~$295/yr at parental-leave-only coverage" figure, `distinctiveFact` rewritten to a new factual claim ("payroll deduction began Jan 1, 2025, not 2026"), and the `ebglaw.com` source citation dropped so only `labor.delaware.gov` remains. Delaware is not in the authorized list (alaska, minnesota, new-jersey, pennsylvania, washington). The external-host diff across `dist` is exactly one line: `168d167 < https://www.ebglaw.com`. The $295 renders on `/delaware-paycheck-calculator/` and twice on `/what-applies-to-me/`.
5. **Garbled visible string shipped on /what-applies-to-me/.** The new Delaware `employeeRate2026` ends in a full stop and the template joins rate and cap with ", ", producing "...seasonal operations that shut down for a month or more are exempt., $184,500 (2026 Social Security wage cap), counting only FICA wages earned in Delaware...". `grep -rl 'exempt\., '` over `dist` returns exactly this one file; it appears twice on the page. The Delaware calculator page escapes it only because the two fields land in separate `<td>` cells.
6. **New on this branch: all 51 state pages assert the study uses "the 2026 rules of every state and DC".** `src/templates/state-page.html:328` is added in this diff (`git show main:src/templates/state-page.html | rg -c "rules of every state"` returns 0, HEAD returns 1). California and Oklahoma are on 2025 rules. For Oklahoma the claim is flatly false rather than pending: the data records HB2764 signed 2025-05-28 cutting the top rate 4.75% to 4.50% effective Jan 1 2026, while the study computes Oklahoma at 4.75%. The Oklahoma state page admits it ("2026 tax will be somewhat lower than shown"); the new blurb does not.
7. **The study page lede contradicts its own corrected byline on the same screen.** `src/templates/data-take-home-pay-by-state.html:138` renders "We ran two identical salaries, $75,000 and $100,000, through the 2026 tax rules of all 50 states and the District of Columbia", directly under a byline saying "2026 except for the states marked in the table". Pre-existing on main.
8. **The CC-BY exports carry no stale-year disclosure.** `dist/data/take-home-pay-by-state-2026.json` is titled tax year 2026, `taxYear "2026"`, license CC-BY-4.0; `/figureYear|2025/.test(JSON.stringify(j))` is false for the whole file, and the `.csv` contains zero occurrences of "2025". Its California record is `{"rank":47,...,"stateIncomeTax":2928,...}` with no `figureYear`. Both are linked twice from the page body and declared as `DataDownload` in the Dataset JSON-LD. Pre-existing on main.
9. **The "ranking is not the same at both salaries" block lists only fallers, and cannot structurally list a riser.** Shipped text names DC (38 to 46), South Carolina (14 to 20), Vermont (27 to 33) and New Jersey (34 to 39) "falling". Computed from the page's own JSON, 14 states move 3 or more ranks and five climb: Kentucky 22 to 18, Michigan 31 to 27, Illinois 41 to 37, Massachusetts 45 to 41, Pennsylvania 18 to 15. In `build.js` the `.slice(0, 4)` runs before the fell/rose split, so the `rose` branch is dead code on this dataset. Rank movement is zero-sum, so the reader is left believing a higher salary only pushes states down.
